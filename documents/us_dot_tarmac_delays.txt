WHAT IS A TARMAC DELAY
A tarmac delay occurs when an aircraft is on the ground, either waiting to take off or after landing, and passengers do not have an opportunity to get off the plane. The DOT tarmac-delay rule applies to covered carriers at U.S. airports. Waiting in the terminal or at a gate is not, by itself, a tarmac delay.

OPPORTUNITY TO LEAVE THE AIRCRAFT
For flights departing from a U.S. airport, airlines must begin moving the aircraft to a place where passengers can safely get off before 3 hours for domestic flights and before 4 hours for international flights. For arriving flights landing at U.S. airports, passengers must be given an opportunity to safely get off before 3 hours for domestic flights and before 4 hours for international flights.

Exceptions to the time limits are allowed for safety, security, or air-traffic-control reasons. Passengers should not leave the aircraft unless the airline tells them it is safe. The airline is not required to let a passenger back on the aircraft after the passenger chooses to get off during a tarmac delay.

FOOD AND DRINKING WATER
During a covered tarmac delay, airlines must provide a snack and drinking water no later than 2 hours after the start of the tarmac delay. The rule does not require a full meal during a tarmac delay. Food and water may be withheld only when the pilot determines service cannot be provided for safety or security reasons.

OTHER PROTECTIONS AND UPDATES
During a tarmac delay, airlines must provide working toilets, comfortable cabin temperatures, and adequate medical attention when needed. Airlines must notify passengers about the status of the delay when the tarmac delay exceeds 30 minutes. If passengers can safely leave, the airline must timely notify them of the opportunity.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
The 3-hour domestic and 4-hour international limits concern the opportunity to leave an aircraft during a covered tarmac delay. They do not mean that every 3- or 4-hour delay while waiting in an airport terminal automatically creates a federal right to a hotel or meal under the tarmac-delay rule. For terminal delays, check the airline's customer-service commitments and the separate DOT refund rules.
