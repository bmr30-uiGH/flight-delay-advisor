SCOPE AND HOW TO APPLY THIS SUMMARY
This document summarizes U.S. DOT guidance. Do not apply these U.S. rules automatically to a flight that is not covered by the U.S. rules. Check whether the itinerary is a covered scheduled flight to, from, or within the United States and whether the airline or ticket agent is the merchant of record for the payment. The merchant of record is generally the entity shown on the passenger's payment statement.

REFUND AFTER A CANCELLATION
For a covered flight cancelled by the airline, a consumer who chooses not to travel or not to accept an offered travel credit, voucher, or other compensation may be entitled to a refund of the ticket price. The reason for the cancellation does not by itself remove the refund right when the consumer chooses not to travel. Apply the detailed DOT and eCFR conditions, including the merchant-of-record rules.

SIGNIFICANT DELAY OR SCHEDULE CHANGE — LATE ARRIVAL
For a domestic itinerary, the late arrival test is met when, because of a delay or change made by the covered carrier, the consumer is scheduled to arrive at the destination airport 3 hours or more after the original scheduled arrival time. For an international itinerary, the late arrival threshold is 6 hours or more after the original scheduled arrival time.

IMPORTANT: compare the ORIGINAL SCHEDULED ARRIVAL with the REVISED SCHEDULED ARRIVAL communicated by the airline. The rule's late-arrival definition uses when the consumer is scheduled to arrive. A flight that actually lands 3.5 hours late, without information showing that the airline's revised schedule met the scheduled-arrival threshold, does not by itself establish this particular significant-change trigger. Do not silently substitute actual arrival time for revised scheduled arrival time.

A departure that is 3 or 4 hours late is not the same as a late-arrival trigger. A delayed departure may or may not result in a scheduled arrival that is at least 3 hours later. Assess the revised scheduled arrival separately.

OTHER SIGNIFICANT CHANGES
The DOT definition also includes certain other carrier-made itinerary changes, including:
- Scheduled early departure at least 3 hours earlier for a domestic itinerary or at least 6 hours earlier for an international itinerary.
- A different origin airport or destination airport.
- More connection points than the original itinerary.
- An involuntary downgrade to a lower class of service.
- Certain changes to connecting airports or aircraft accessibility features for a passenger with a disability.
Check the detailed rule for the precise conditions. Early departure means leaving earlier than originally scheduled; it does not mean a departure that is late.

NON-REFUNDABLE TICKET — REFUND WHEN THE PASSENGER DOES NOT TRAVEL
14 CFR §260.6(a) specifically addresses a consumer holding a nonrefundable ticket on a covered scheduled flight to, from, or within the United States. A covered carrier that is the merchant of record must provide a full and prompt refund of airfare, including applicable taxes and ancillary fees, when the flight is cancelled or significantly delayed/changed and the consumer chooses not to fly on the affected flight or accept rebooking on an alternative flight, and does not accept a voucher, credit, or other compensation offered as an explicit alternative to the refund.

Do not state that every passenger with a non-refundable ticket automatically receives a refund after any delay. First establish that the flight was cancelled or significantly delayed/changed under the applicable definition and that the consumer did not take the affected or alternative transportation or accept compensation in place of the refund.

IF THE PASSENGER REJECTS AN OFFER OR DOES NOT RESPOND
Under the automatic-refund provisions, a refund is due in the circumstances described by the rule, including when the consumer rejects the significantly delayed/changed flight, alternative flight, or offered compensation. If the consumer does not respond to an offer of the changed/alternative flight and that flight departs without the consumer, or does not respond to a voucher/credit/compensation offer by the relevant scheduled departure date, the automatic-refund provisions may apply. Silence alone must not be described as an automatic refund without checking the precise offer and departure conditions.

If a passenger intends to travel on the changed or rebooked flight, the passenger should tell the airline before the response deadline stated in the offer. Do not infer acceptance or rejection from incomplete facts.

INVOLUNTARY DOWNGRADE — CONTINUED TRAVEL AND FARE DIFFERENCE
The U.S. DOT Refunds page identifies an involuntary move to a lower class of service as a significant change. If the passenger chooses not to travel or not to accept travel credits, vouchers, or other compensation offered by the airline because of the involuntary downgrade, the DOT page states that a refund may be due under the applicable conditions.

If the downgraded passenger continues to travel on the flight, the U.S. DOT states that the passenger is not entitled to a refund of the full airfare, but the airline must refund the difference between the original fare and the downgraded fare. This is a fare-difference remedy, not a full ticket refund.

The U.S. DOT Refunds page's downgrade explanation does not state a separate processing deadline for this fare-difference refund. Do not automatically apply the 7-business-day or 20-calendar-day prompt-refund deadlines for a ticket-price refund to the fare difference unless a retrieved source expressly establishes that the deadline applies to this remedy. Do not invent a fare difference amount or a processing deadline.

IF THE PASSENGER ACCEPTS OR USES THE CHANGED FLIGHT
A consumer who chooses to take the significantly delayed/changed flight or an alternative flight offered by the airline generally is not entitled to a refund of the full ticket price under the DOT significant-delay/change refund rule. Do not tell a passenger who completed the changed or alternative trip that they automatically qualify for a full ticket refund merely because the actual arrival was late.

A completed journey does not rule out every other possible remedy. For example, when a passenger is involuntarily downgraded and continues to travel, DOT guidance says the passenger is generally entitled to the difference between the original fare and the downgraded fare, rather than a refund of the full airfare. Other airline commitments or applicable rules may also be relevant; retrieve a supporting source before stating them.

FULLY REFUNDABLE TICKET
DOT separately states that a consumer who purchases a fully refundable ticket is entitled to a refund when the consumer does not use the purchased ticket. Keep this separate from the specific non-refundable-ticket obligation in 14 CFR §260.6(a). Do not imply that §260.6(a) is written as a rule covering both fare types in the same way. Check the ticket's terms and whether the ticket was used.

DISABILITY-RELATED ACCESSIBILITY CHANGES
14 CFR §260.6(b) contains separate refund provisions for certain significant changes affecting a passenger with a disability and accessibility features needed by that passenger. If the user raises such circumstances, retrieve and explain the relevant provision; do not assume the general non-refundable-ticket rule is the only provision that may apply.

REFUND PAYMENT METHOD AND DEADLINES
A required refund is generally returned to the original form of payment. The regulatory definition of a prompt refund is within 7 business days after the applicable earliest date for a credit-card purchase, and within 20 calendar days for cash, check, debit card, or other payment methods. Never label the 20-day period as a business-day period. Explain the applicable trigger date carefully: for example, DOT guidance distinguishes the date the passenger rejects an offer from the later departure date when the passenger does not respond and does not use the alternative flight.

VOUCHERS, CREDITS, AND OTHER COMPENSATION OFFERED IN PLACE OF A REFUND
A covered carrier may offer a voucher, credit, or other compensation as an explicit alternative to a refund only under the conditions in the regulation. The offer must clearly and conspicuously disclose the consumer's right to a full refund. Such an alternative must remain valid and redeemable for at least five years, and the consumer must be notified of its expiration date. Do not say that the mere mention of a voucher automatically cancels the passenger's refund right. Determine whether the passenger affirmatively accepted the alternative and check the applicable conditions.

TICKETS PURCHASED THROUGH AN AGENT OR ONLINE TRAVEL AGENCY
The merchant of record is the entity shown on the passenger's financial statement for the ticket payment. If an online travel agency or other ticket agent is the merchant of record, that entity may be responsible for issuing the proper refund. Do not automatically direct every passenger to the airline without checking who processed the payment; explain that the passenger may need to contact the merchant of record and the airline.

WHAT THIS RULE DOES NOT ESTABLISH
A refund right is not the same as a right to extra cash compensation. Do not invent a universal cash-compensation amount for a delay. Meals, hotels, ground transportation, rebooking, and other services may depend on separate airline commitments, the cause of the disruption, and other applicable rules. Retrieve the airline's official commitment source before describing a particular airline's promise.


PENOLAKAN EKSPLISIT ATAS TAWARAN PENERBANGAN ALTERNATIF — REFUND OTOMATIS
Untuk penerbangan yang dibatalkan atau mengalami significant delay/change, apabila konsumen secara eksplisit menolak penerbangan yang berubah atau rebooking pada penerbangan alternatif, refund harus diterbitkan otomatis tanpa konsumen harus mengajukan permintaan refund tambahan. Kondisi penolakan eksplisit ini sesuai dengan 14 CFR §260.6(a)(2)(ii). Jangan mengutip §260.6(a)(2)(iii)(A) untuk penolakan eksplisit; subbagian (iii)(A) adalah kondisi berbeda ketika konsumen tidak merespons tawaran flight dan flight yang ditawarkan berangkat tanpa konsumen.

TENGGAT SETELAH PENOLAKAN EKSPLISIT
Halaman resmi U.S. DOT menyatakan bahwa refund jatuh tempo dalam 7 business days setelah konsumen menolak tawaran jika tiket dibayar dengan kartu kredit, atau 20 calendar days setelah penolakan jika pembayaran memakai metode lain. In other words, the DOT describes these deadlines as after the consumer rejects the offer. Gunakan tanggal ketika penolakan disampaikan kepada pihak yang memproses tawaran sebagai trigger penolakan eksplisit. Jangan menunggu sampai penerbangan alternatif berangkat pada skenario penolakan eksplisit. Trigger keberangkatan penerbangan adalah aturan yang berbeda untuk konsumen yang tidak merespons tawaran alternatif.

TIDAK MERESPONS BUKAN SAMA DENGAN MENOLAK
Untuk konsumen yang tidak merespons tawaran penerbangan yang berubah atau alternatif, 14 CFR §260.6(a)(2)(iii)(A) mensyaratkan bahwa flight yang ditawarkan berangkat tanpa konsumen. Untuk konsumen yang tidak merespons voucher/credit, §260.6(a)(2)(iii)(B) menggunakan tanggal berbeda: tanggal ketika flight yang dibatalkan semula dijadwalkan berangkat atau tanggal flight yang significantly delayed/changed berangkat. Jangan menyamakan kedua kondisi tersebut atau menyimpulkan bahwa setiap bentuk diam otomatis memicu refund.

MERCHANT OF RECORD DAN PEMESANAN MELALUI AGEN/OTA
Merchant of record adalah entitas (carrier atau ticket agent) yang memproses pembayaran tiket dan tercantum pada financial charge statement, misalnya mutasi kartu debit/kredit. Bank atau penerbit kartu bukan merchant of record hanya karena kartu tersebut digunakan untuk membayar. Merchant of record dapat berupa maskapai atau ticket agent/online travel agency (OTA).

JALUR REFUND JIKA MASKAPAI ADALAH MERCHANT OF RECORD
Untuk tiket yang dibeli langsung dari maskapai, U.S. DOT menjelaskan bahwa ketika maskapai membatalkan atau mengubah penerbangan secara signifikan dan konsumen menolak alternatif/kompensasi, refund harus diterbitkan otomatis tanpa konsumen mengajukan permintaan refund terpisah. Untuk kartu kredit, DOT menyebut 7 business days setelah penolakan eksplisit; untuk metode pembayaran lain, 20 calendar days setelah penolakan eksplisit.

JALUR REFUND JIKA TICKET AGENT/OTA ADALAH MERCHANT OF RECORD
14 CFR §399.80(l) mewajibkan ticket agent yang menjadi merchant of record memberikan refund prompt ATAS PERMINTAAN konsumen untuk penerbangan yang dibatalkan atau berubah signifikan, jika konsumen memilih tidak bepergian atau tidak menerima kompensasi pengganti refund sesuai kondisi aturan. Karena itu jangan menyatakan proses refund OTA otomatis tanpa permintaan konsumen. DOT menyarankan konsumen yang membeli melalui OTA/agen menghubungi agen tersebut untuk memperoleh refund. Untuk pembelian kartu kredit, refund prompt dari ticket agent harus dilakukan dalam 7 business days sejak ticket agent menerima informasi dari carrier sebagaimana §260.6(d), sesuai 12 CFR Part 1026. Untuk pembayaran cash, check, debit card, atau metode lain, §399.80(l) menetapkan 20 calendar days sejak refund menjadi jatuh tempo. Refund dikembalikan ke bentuk pembayaran awal, kecuali konsumen setuju menerima bentuk lain yang cash-equivalent.

Berdasarkan 14 CFR §260.6(d), setelah konsumen mengajukan permintaan refund melalui ticket agent, carrier yang membatalkan/mengubah penerbangan harus memberi tahu ticket agent tanpa penundaan apakah konsumen memenuhi syarat refund. Berdasarkan §260.6(e), carrier yang memegang dana harus segera mentransfer dana kepada ticket agent jika agent bertanggung jawab mengeluarkan refund tetapi tidak memegang dana tersebut. Jangan menganggap bank/penerbit kartu sebagai merchant of record.

FACTS NEEDED FOR A CASE-SPECIFIC ASSESSMENT
When these details are missing and they materially affect the answer, establish: (1) whether the itinerary is covered by U.S. DOT rules; (2) whether the airline cancelled or changed the schedule; (3) the original and revised scheduled arrival times, or the other relevant significant change; (4) whether the passenger flew on the changed flight or an alternative flight, rejected it, or did not respond and did not travel; (5) whether the ticket was non-refundable or fully refundable; and (6) who was the merchant of record. Do not ask for information that is not needed to answer the user's question.

FULLY REFUNDABLE TICKET — PROCESSING DEADLINE LIMITS
The U.S. DOT Refunds page states that consumers who purchase fully refundable tickets are entitled to a refund when they do not use the purchased ticket. Keep this rule separate from 14 CFR §260.6(a)(1), which specifically addresses a consumer holding a nonrefundable ticket on a covered scheduled flight that is cancelled or significantly delayed/changed and who chooses not to travel or accept an alternative or compensation in lieu of the refund.

The cited DOT page does not state a separate processing deadline in its fully-refundable-ticket explanation for a passenger who voluntarily does not use the ticket. Do not automatically apply the 7-business-day or 20-calendar-day prompt-refund periods in 14 CFR Part 260 to this scenario unless another retrieved source expressly establishes that they apply. Do not invent a deadline.

This section concerns the ticket price. Do not assume that every optional ancillary service fee (for example, baggage, seat selection, or lounge access) is automatically refundable under the fully-refundable-ticket statement. Check the separate DOT guidance for the particular ancillary service.

AUTOMATIC REFUND — NO RESPONSE TO AN ALTERNATIVE-FLIGHT OFFER
Under 14 CFR §260.6(a)(2)(iii)(A), when a consumer does not respond to an offer of a significantly delayed or changed flight or an alternative flight, the automatic-refund condition includes that the offered flight departs without the consumer. Do not state that silence alone is sufficient before checking that departure condition and that the underlying flight qualifies for a refund under the applicable rule.

The U.S. DOT Refunds page explains that when the consumer does not respond to an alternative-transportation offer and does not fly on the offered alternative, the refund is due to the original form of payment within 7 business days for credit-card payments or 20 calendar days for other payment methods after the significantly delayed or changed flight or the alternative flight departs from its destination. For an explicit rejection, the DOT page describes the deadline as running from the date the consumer rejects the offer.

A different no-response condition applies to an offer of voucher, credit, or other compensation. Under 14 CFR §260.6(a)(2)(iii)(B), check whether the consumer failed to respond by the date on which the cancelled flight was scheduled to depart or the date the significantly delayed or changed flight departs. Do not reuse the alternative-flight departure trigger for this voucher/credit scenario.
